# Simulated behaviour-response study

> **SIMULATION ONLY. NOT CLINICAL EVIDENCE.** Virtual patients from the held-out test split follow the patient app's lifestyle suggestions inside the same simulator that generated the training data. This checks that the closed loop works mechanically. It does not show that real people would benefit.

Setup: 12 patients x 3 simulated days, paired arms (identical meals, sleep and sensor noise). Suggestions are evaluated every 30 min with the served model and the app's next-action rules. Following means a 15-min brisk walk, or roti/millet instead of rice at the next meal (-30% carbs, slower absorption).

**How to read this.** *Time > 180* is the more stable outcome. *Spike onsets* counts sustained crossings above 180 mg/dL, and a walk in the middle of an excursion can split one long episode into two short ones. So onset counts need not fall monotonically with adherence, even when total time above range does. TBR (time below 70) is reported to check that the suggestions do not create hypoglycaemia in the simulator. Very few patients and days: treat this as a mechanism check only.
